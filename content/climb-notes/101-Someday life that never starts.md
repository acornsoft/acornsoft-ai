## Problem

You keep a private list of “someday.” Learn the language. Fix the money loop. Call the friend. Start the small business. Write the story your kids will actually hear. None of it is frivolous — and almost none of it gets a first pitch. The week always has something more urgent. Someday stays a folder of good intentions, not a path you can stand on.

That is not a character flaw. It is what happens when hope has no trail map. Without a problem you can say out loud, a way to know you started, a small slice you can finish this week, and a lesson you can reuse — someday never becomes a climb. It becomes guilt you carry and never convert into motion.

## Measure

One real “someday” item moves into this week with a finished first slice — not a reinvention of your life, not a thirty-day overhaul. Something you can point to and say: this existed only as a wish on Monday; by Sunday it has a first footprint on the mountain.

## Slice

Name one someday out loud. Write it as a Climb Note in plain language:

1. **Problem** — what hurts or stalls when this stays a wish.
2. **Measure** — how you will know the first step happened.
3. **Slice** — the smallest move this week that still counts as real.
4. **Lesson** — what you will carry so next week is not another blank page.

Share it with one person if you want accountability — or keep it private. The point is not polish. The point is a trail you can find again.

## Lesson

Someday becomes real when it has a trail map. Climb Notes turn vague hope into a path you can climb again — on the same mountain path next week, or on a completely new path when life turns. You do not free-solo your future. You climb with a guide, and you leave notes for the next pitch.
