## Problem

People cannot tell if a teammate is free, in a meeting, or “green but unavailable.” Calendar is one system; chat is another; the real answer lives in someone’s head. Leaders ask for “presence that means something,” then get a status dot that lies. Without a product climb—problem, measure, slice, lesson—Teams Presence stays a feature request that never compounds into a shippable trail.

## Measure

A small group can answer in under five seconds: is this person free to talk *for this purpose* (quick ping, deep work, client call)? Success is not a prettier status UI. Success is fewer “are you free?” messages and one written Climb Note that names what we shipped and what we learned.

## Slice

Ship a thin vertical for **one** team (not the whole company):

1. **Problem** in this note — presence that lies.
2. **Measure** — five-second “free for what?” answer for that team.
3. **Build** — a Presence surface that reads calendar + explicit “focus / open / client” state (not only green/yellow/red).
4. **Write back** — update this Climb Note with what failed, what stuck, and the next pitch.

Do not boil the ocean of global directory presence. One team, one measure, one lesson.

## Lesson

Presence is a climb about *intent*, not a color. The first slice that earns trust is smaller than the roadmap slide. When the note is honest about what “free” means, the next climb (routing, Luna as Sherpa, or Canopy status) starts from a trail—not a free solo demo.
